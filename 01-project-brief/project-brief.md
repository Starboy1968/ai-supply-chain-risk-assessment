# Project brief - AI Supply Chain Risk Assessment

*Ratings are analyst judgment for an illustrative mid-size manufacturer/distributor, not measured data. Sources checked October 2026.*

## Objective

Identify, score and prioritize the new risks that AI tools introduce across a manufacturer/distributor's supply chain, and recommend specific, owned controls a GRC or third-party risk team could start on within 90 days.

## Questions the project set out to answer

1. Where in the supply chain is AI already in use (often embedded in vendor software), and where is it being used informally?
2. Which AI-related risks are most likely and most damaging for this kind of organization?
3. Which specific controls reduce those risks, and who should own them?
4. What should we ask AI vendors before and during the relationship?
5. How do these risks map to the frameworks auditors and customers ask about (NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, NIST CSF 2.0, NIST SP 800-161r1, EU AI Act, OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications)?

## Scope

| In scope | Out of scope |
|---|---|
| AI used by or on behalf of the company across plan, source, make, deliver, return and enabling functions | Physical supply risks with no AI link (weather, port strikes, raw-material shortages) |
| Generative AI tools used by staff (approved and unapproved) | Building or training frontier AI models |
| AI embedded in SaaS (ERP, TMS, WMS, procurement) and its upstream model providers | Product safety certification of AI inside the company's own products |
| AI agents that read supplier emails and documents | Detailed legal advice (legal points are flagged for counsel) |
| Attacker use of AI against the supply chain (deepfakes, AI-written fraud) | Quantified loss modeling (listed as a next step) |

## Illustrative organization profile

*Fictional, used to anchor the ratings.*

- US-based manufacturer and distributor of industrial components; about 1,200 employees, 3 plants, 2 distribution centers.
- About 400 active suppliers in roughly a dozen countries; sells to customers in the US and the EU.
- Cloud ERP with built-in AI forecasting; TMS and WMS with AI optimization features; AI-assisted supplier research in procurement.
- Piloting an AI agent that triages the accounts payable inbox and extracts invoice data.
- No formal AI policy yet; staff use public AI chatbots informally.
- Has a basic third-party risk program (security questionnaires for IT vendors) but no AI-specific questions.
